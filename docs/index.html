Quantum Tunneling

Numerical Study of the 1D Schrödinger Equation
scroll ↓

Abstract

We present a comparative numerical study of two methods for solving the one-dimensional time-dependent Schrödinger equation: the Crank-Nicolson (CN) finite-difference scheme and the Split-Step Fourier (SSF) spectral method. Both are validated against analytical results for quantum tunneling through a rectangular potential barrier.

Key finding: CN achieves higher accuracy for discontinuous potentials, while SSF is 2–4× faster but suffers from Gibbs oscillations at potential edges.

Physical Model

Governing Equation

\[i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} = \left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2} + V(x)\right]\psi\]

In dimensionless units (\(\hbar = 1\), \(m = 1\)):

\[i\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} = \left[-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2} + V(x)\right]\psi\]

Initial Condition: Gaussian Wave Packet

\[\psi(x, 0) = \frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^2)^{1/4}} \exp\left(-\frac{(x - x_0)^2}{4\sigma^2}\right) \exp(ik_0 x)\]

Potential: Rectangular Barrier

\[V(x) = \begin{cases} V_0 & \text{for } x \in [x_b, x_b + a] \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}\]

Numerical Methods

Crank-Nicolson Method

Implicit finite-difference, second-order accurate in space and time.

\[A\psi^{n+1} = B\psi^n\] \[A = I + \frac{i\Delta t}{2}H, \quad B = I - \frac{i\Delta t}{2}H\]

Split-Step Fourier Method

Operator splitting with FFT (Strang splitting).

\[\psi \leftarrow \psi \cdot e^{-iV\Delta t/2}\] \[\psi \leftarrow \text{IFFT}\left(\text{FFT}(\psi) \cdot e^{-ik^2\Delta t/2}\right)\] \[\psi \leftarrow \psi \cdot e^{-iV\Delta t/2}\]

Validation

Norm Conservation

Both methods conserve total probability with error < 1e-13 over 400 time steps.

Limitations

  • Model is one-dimensional
  • Non-relativistic quantum mechanics only
  • Non-interacting particles
  • Simple rectangular barrier (most experiments)
  • Finite grid introduces numerical error
  • Wave packet has energy spread (not truly monochromatic)
